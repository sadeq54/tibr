# 03 · 21K vs 18K — two karats side by side

## Main · Two bangles rotating

```
Vertical macro cinematography of two identical gold bangles standing on edge side by side on dark charcoal velvet, separated by a narrow band of untouched velvet running down the exact centre of the frame. The bangle on the left is a deeper, richer, more saturated yellow gold; the bangle on the right is noticeably paler and cooler, closer to a soft champagne gold. They are identical in shape, thickness and finish, so the only difference the eye can find is the colour of the metal. Both rotate together very slowly, less than twenty degrees in total across the shot, so the light rolls across their curved surfaces and the tonal difference between the two metals becomes unmistakable. Camera is locked on a tripod, completely static, no push and no handheld drift. Shot on a 100mm macro lens at f/3.5, deep enough that both bangles hold sharpness across their curve. Key light is a broad soft warm source at 3400K positioned high and slightly behind, producing a clean specular arc along the top of each band. A weak cool fill at 5600K from low front at one-tenth intensity opens the shadow inside each bangle. The velvet swallows light and reads almost black. Deep vignette. Colour palette limited to two distinct golds, charcoal and near-black. Perfect left-right symmetry, the centre line of the frame falling exactly in the gap. Fine film grain, photorealistic, jewellery-catalogue quality. Upper half of the vertical frame stays empty and dark for a text overlay. One continuous take, no cuts.
```
